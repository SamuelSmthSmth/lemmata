Getting started

Lemmata checks a proof one line at a time. You write the proof the way your notes write it; each step goes to a computer algebra system (SymPy) or a logic solver (Z3), and the first step that does not follow is marked, with the reason and — where one exists — a concrete counterexample.

Your first proof

A proof is a list of statements. Let or Given introduces variables, Assume a hypothesis, Step: a calculation, and Therefore a conclusion. Leave out the left-hand side of a Step: to continue the chain from the previous one.

Theorem: "Even square theorem"
Proof:
    Given n : Int
    Assume h1: Even(n)
    Obtain k : Int such that n = 2 * k from h1
    Step: n^2 = (2 * k)^2
    Step: = 4 * k^2
    Step: = 2 * (2 * k^2)
    Therefore exists m : Int, n^2 = 4 * m [witness: k^2]
    Hence MultipleOf(n^2, 4)
QED

Reading the result

The status bar at the foot of the window gives the verdict for the whole proof: VALID, VALID (with domain warnings), INVALID or PARSE ERROR. The auditor lists every statement with what checked it; a step that fails carries a red rule in the margin, and so does everything after it, because everything after it rests on it. The editor marks the same line in its gutter, and hovering the mark shows the reason.

Click any line — in the editor or in the auditor — and Context & state shows what the checker knows at that point: the variables in scope with their types, and every hypothesis and derived fact.

When a step is wrong

Let x : Real
Step: (x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 1

The auditor reports the step as invalid and gives a value of x where the two sides differ. A counterexample is the most useful thing a checker can hand you: it shows not just that the step is wrong, but where to look.

Where your work lives

Everything is kept in this browser: open proofs, folders, snapshots and settings. Use Files in the reading pane to organise proofs, and the export button there to download the whole workspace as a .zip — that file is your backup. Dropping .aether files or a .zip anywhere on the window adds them back.

Working from the notes

The Library holds your installed packs: proofs transcribed from a module's lecture notes, keyed to the notes' own numbering, such as MTH2008 · Example 2.18. Opening one keeps the entry beside a copy of its proof in the Notes tab while you work on it. Traps are deliberate mistakes; open one as an exercise and find the failing line before the checker tells you. Use in a proof imports a proved theorem into your own proof, and packs install, update and export like packages; New pack… makes one from a folder of your proofs.
