From here to Lean

Lean is a proof assistant: it checks a proof down to the axioms, with no solver to trust. Show in Lean, at the end of the tool strip, states your proof in Lean 4 with Mathlib, side by side with your own lines, so you can see what each line becomes and take it from there.

Each step tries its rule

Each step’s proof tries the Lean tactic for the rule Lemmata checked it by: ring for algebra, linarith or nlinarith for inequalities, group in a group, and so on, written first | (ring; done) | … | sorry. Where Lean’s tactic proves the step, the step is proved; where it cannot, the step falls back to sorry, a gap Lean accepts with a warning. The comment beside it names the rule. Across every pinned proof, Lean proves about three steps in five this way. Calculus steps stay sorry: Lean’s deriv and limits need lemmas, not one tactic. A step that did not check keeps its red rail and says so: its Lean states what you claimed, which is not true.

What has no faithful Lean, such as arithmetic with ∞ or an indefinite integral, is left as sorry too and named above the table. It is never guessed.

Taking it further

Open in Lean’s web editor loads the skeleton in Lean’s own editor in your browser, Mathlib included: see which steps Lean already proves, and fill each remaining sorry yourself. Download .lean gives you the file for a Lean project of your own. Every example, every pack entry and every example in this Guide is compiled against Mathlib whenever the checker changes, so the skeleton compiles as given.
