What the checker can decide

Lemmata is honest about where it stops. When it cannot decide a step it says so, rather than presenting a solver artefact as a counterexample.

Where it stops

  • Transcendental functions. The solver knows the ranges of exp, log, sin and cos, but not the functions themselves. An inequality that needs more than the range — exp(x) ≥ 1 + x + x²/2 for x ≥ 0 — cannot be decided, and the message says so. Equalities about them are settled by algebra and do work.
  • Hard non-linear questions. Every check has a time budget (20 seconds). A step that asks something the solver cannot settle quickly is stopped and reported as TIMEOUT; split it into smaller steps, or add the hypothesis it depends on.
  • Sets and topology. Membership, subsets, unions and intersections work; set literals {a, b, c}, sup and inf as operators, and open covers are not part of the language yet.
  • Powers of group elements. Literal powers (a^3, a^-1) expand; a symbolic power a^n of a group element stays opaque.
