Algebra

Groups, subgroups, homomorphisms and modular arithmetic, in the notes' own multiplicative notation.

Groups and their elements

Assume Group(G, op, e, inv) makes G a group with operation op, identity e and inverse inv. After that, G is a type: Given a, b : G declares elements, and ∀ x ∈ G, … quantifies over them.

For elements, a * b (or a·b) means op(a, b), a^-1 (or a⁻¹) means inv(a), and a^3 means a·a·a. Variables of a number type keep ordinary, commuting multiplication.

Assume Group(G, op, e, inv)
Given a, b : G
Step: (a·b)⁻¹ = b⁻¹·a⁻¹
Step: a²·a³ = a⁵
Step: a·a⁻¹ = e

Identities that hold in every group

An equation built only from the group's operations is checked as an identity of every group, so it is not misled by commutativity. ab = ba is refused in a Group — the two sides are different words — and accepted in an AbelianGroup.

Assume Group(G, op, e, inv)
Given a, b : G
Step: (a * b)^-1 = a^-1 * b^-1

Using hypotheses

Theorem: "Left cancellation"
Proof:
    Assume Group(G, op, e, inv)
    Given a, u, v : G
    Assume h: op(a, u) = op(a, v)
    Step: u = op(inv(a), op(a, u))
    Step: = op(inv(a), op(a, v))
    Step: = v
QED

Subgroups and normal subgroups

Subgroup(H, G, op, e, inv) and NormalSubgroup(N, G, op, e, inv) add closure, identity and (for a normal subgroup) conjugation. H becomes a type as well.

Assume Group(G, op, e, inv)
Assume Subgroup(H, G, op, e, inv)
Given x, y, z : G
Assume h1: op(inv(x), y) in H
Assume h2: op(inv(y), z) in H
Step: op(op(inv(x), y), op(inv(y), z)) = op(inv(x), z)
Therefore op(inv(x), z) in H

Modular arithmetic

Congruences are checked over the integers, including a symbolic modulus: the congruences you assume are turned into witnesses (i − k = n·t) before the goal is tested. Modulo 0, congruence is equality.

Let i, j, k, l, n : Int
Assume hk: i = k (mod n)
Assume hl: j = l (mod n)
Therefore i + j = k + l (mod n)
